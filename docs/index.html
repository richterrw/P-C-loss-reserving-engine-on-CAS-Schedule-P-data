Why they disagree

The gap between chain ladder and BF is leverage times surprise

Every expected-loss method blends the chain ladder answer with a prior. One line of algebra shows where the disagreement comes from:

U_CL − U_BF = (1 − 1/CDF) × (U_CL − U_prior) leverage surprise

For State Farm other liability, accident year 1997, only $21M had been paid and the development factor was 30.6. Chain ladder projected $645M. BF projected $307M. The actual was $329M. Ninety percent of that company's gap sat in its three newest accident years.

Paid vs. incurred

Paid chain ladder assumes settlement speed is stable. Claims were paid faster through the 1990s, so recent years looked more developed than they were and paid projections overshot.

The prior

Cape Cod uses one flat loss ratio. When a book's loss ratios trend down, as State Farm auto's did from 87% to 68%, a flat prior overstates every recent year.

Case reserves

A med mal insurer's incurred losses fell as over-set case reserves were released. Incurred methods tracked it; paid chain ladder missed by 48%; the company's own booking was 96% high.

Reconciliation has limits

Munich chain ladder pulls paid toward incurred and closes the gap between them from 3.9% to 0.8%. Where case reserves were wrong, it inherits their error.

Uncertainty

The ranges were too confident

A calibrated model puts 10% of actual outcomes below its 10th percentile and 10% above its 90th. On paid data, roughly 28% fell below: the mean was biased, and no amount of extra spread fixes that. Shapland's bootstrap refinements came closest on incurred data, at a third less width than the plain bootstrap.
